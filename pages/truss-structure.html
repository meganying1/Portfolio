Truss Structure

Designing and fabricating a lightweight truss structure to withstand a 40-pound load.

Overview

In a team of three, I helped design and fabricate a lightweight acrylic truss for a 40-pound center load. We developed the structure through concept generation, structural analysis, CAD, fabrication, and destructive testing.

Each team member developed three candidate designs and performed member-force calculations for their own concepts. We then compared the strongest candidates and selected the geometry with the best predicted strength-to-weight performance for further development.

Concept Development

We began by having each team member develop three candidate truss configurations. We compared the nine concepts based on geometry, expected load paths, and structural efficiency, then selected the configuration that provided the best balance between load capacity and material usage.

After selecting the final geometry, we modeled the complete assembly in SolidWorks to verify dimensions and prepare the design for fabrication.

Engineering Analysis

For the selected design, we used static equilibrium to calculate the internal forces in each member under the 40-pound center load. The highest calculated loads were approximately 28.3 lb in compression in the outer diagonal members. We used these forces and the acrylic material properties to determine the required member dimensions and estimate the total weight of the structure.

Fabrication & Testing

After finalizing the design, we generated DXF files from the CAD model and prepared the parts for laser cutting in CorelDRAW. We then fabricated the acrylic members and assembled the truss using screws and bolts.

Our first prototype used 1/16-inch-thick members and failed at approximately 20 lb, well below the target. We increased the thickness to 5/32 inch, after which the next prototype supported more than 50 lb without failing. We then reduced the thickness to 3/32 inch to move the failure load closer to the 40-pound target.

Results

The final truss failed at approximately 38 lb, close to the 40-pound design target, and failed in the member location predicted by our structural analysis.
