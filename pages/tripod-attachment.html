Tripod Attachment

Designing an assistive tripod attachment that automates phone mounting and reduces force and dexterity needed for positioning.

Overview

In a team of three, I helped develop an assistive tripod attachment for users with cerebral palsy and wrist tendonitis. We took the design from user research and early ideation through prototyping, stress analysis, manufacturing planning, and cost estimation.

The final design uses an IR sensor and servo-driven TPU wings to secure the phone automatically, along with a rounded toothed interface that allows the phone to rotate with less force and precision than a standard tripod adjustment mechanism.

User-Centered Design

We began by identifying the most difficult interactions in existing tripods. User research highlighted challenges with small mounting screws, adjustment mechanisms, gripping, and precise positioning.

From these findings, we focused the design on two primary functions: automatically securing the phone and reducing the effort required to adjust its orientation.

During early ideation, I helped develop the concept of using an IR sensor to detect the phone and trigger servo-driven gripping wings. We also explored a rotating base interface that would allow the phone to be repositioned without repeatedly loosening and tightening a conventional tripod mechanism.

As the design developed, we added foam to accommodate different phone sizes, changed the gripping wings to TPU for greater compliance, and refined the rotating interface with rounded teeth to reduce the force required for adjustment.

Mechanical Design

The phone rests in a padded base between two flexible wings connected to micro servos. When the IR sensor detects a phone, the servos rotate the wings inward to secure it without requiring the user to tighten a clamp.

We selected TPU for the wings to combine flexibility with enough stiffness to hold the phone securely. Cutouts along each wing increase compliance and allow the geometry to accommodate different phone sizes and orientations.

Beneath the phone holder, a two-piece toothed interface controls rotational positioning. The inner interface uses flexible TPU, while the outer component is rigid plastic. The rounded teeth deform during adjustment, then engage to hold the selected position.

Engineering Analysis

I analyzed the TPU gripping wings to verify that they could withstand the load applied by the servos while securing the phone. Using the servo torque and wing geometry, I estimated a 2.97 N load on each wing. My hand calculations predicted a maximum bending stress of approximately 2.45 MPa, corresponding to a factor of safety of 21.4.

We then evaluated the wing using FEA under the same loading condition. The simulation predicted a maximum stress of 10.2 MPa and a factor of safety of 5.1. We attributed the difference between the analytical and FEA results primarily to stress concentrations and geometric simplifications in the hand calculations.

Fabrication & Testing

We fabricated the prototype using 3D-printed components and off-the-shelf electronics, including micro servos, an IR sensor, magnets, and an Arduino Nano.

The assembled prototype allowed us to evaluate the gripping mechanism, phone fit, and rotating interface. Testing revealed that the glued connection between the TPU wings and servo horns was not sufficiently robust, and the foam-lined phone slot was too narrow for the phone to slide fully into place.

These results identified clear improvements for the next iteration, including redesigning the wing-to-servo interface and widening the phone slot.

Manufacturing & Cost

For higher-volume production, we selected materials and manufacturing methods based on the functional requirements of each component. We specified TPU for the wings and inner rotating interface, with rigid plastic for the base and outer interface. Injection molding was selected for the molded components at production scale.

Using supplier quotations at a sample quantity of 50 units, we estimated a total production cost of approximately $58.18 per unit and a proposed selling price of $62.25.

Results

The final prototype demonstrated automatic phone gripping and low-force rotational positioning in a standard tripod attachment. Testing also exposed limitations in the wing attachment and phone slot geometry, providing specific design changes for a future iteration.
